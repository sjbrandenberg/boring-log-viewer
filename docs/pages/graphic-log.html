Graphic log and soil symbols

How each layer's symbol is chosen, and every built-in symbol the viewer can draw.

How the symbol is chosen

For each layer, the viewer uses the first of these that applies:

  1. The layer's hatch, if you give one. "hatch": "none" leaves the layer blank.
  2. A material named in the description, such as fill, asphalt, concrete, topsoil, no recovery, or a rock name ("SHALE, black, hard"). Fill describes where the soil came from, so it wins over the soil type: a layer described as "silty SAND (FILL)" with "uscs": "SM" is drawn with the fill symbol, and SM still appears in the USCS column.
  3. The layer's uscs symbol.
  4. A USCS symbol worked out from the description, such as SM from "silty SAND". When the description settles it, the symbol is also shown in the USCS column, in parentheses.
  5. A best guess from keywords in the description, for the graphic log only. For example, "SAND, fine to medium" is drawn as SP, but nothing is shown in the USCS column, because the description doesn't say enough to classify it.
Want full control?

Give every layer a uscs or hatch. To stop materials being picked out of descriptions, turn off infer_materials; to stop inferred symbols appearing in the USCS column, turn off infer_uscs. Both are display options.

When a USCS symbol is inferred

A symbol is shown in the USCS column (in parentheses) only when the description determines it:

  • a symbol written in the text, such as "(SP-SM)";
  • an ASTM D2487 group name, such as "fat CLAY", "silty SAND" or "poorly graded SAND with silt";
  • CLAY or SILT with a plasticity descriptor ("CLAY, high plasticity");
  • "silty clay" (CL-ML), SILT group names (ML), and organic SILT or CLAY with low (OL) or high (OH) plasticity.

Mixed layers, ranges ("lean to fat"), bare SAND or GRAVEL, and "clayey SILT" get no symbol. These rules were checked against 655 layer descriptions from the Next Generation Liquefaction (NGL) database.

When a material is picked out

A material counts only when it is the principal material of the description:

  • Fill: as the main material ("FILL"), as a leading label ("FILL: silty sand"), or as an origin tag ("(HYDRAULIC FILL)"). Hydraulic, engineered and undocumented fill have their own symbols. British "MADE GROUND" is fill too.
  • Pavement and base: asphalt, concrete, aggregate base course.
  • Other materials: topsoil, shells, cobbles and boulders, wood, ash, pumice, loam and others in the table below.
  • No recovery or core loss, when no soil is named.
  • Rock names: "SHALE, black, hard", "Weathered GRANITE". "rock, clay" is claystone.

A minor constituent never counts: "silty SAND with trace shells" is sand. A soil named in capitals wins over a material that isn't: "coarse pumice SAND" is sand. Bentonite is drawn as CH. These rules were checked against 802 NGL layer descriptions.

USCS symbols

The soil symbols follow the legend of the Caltrans Soil and Rock Logging, Classification, and Presentation Manual (2010).

Dual symbols

Coarse-grained dual symbols (5 to 12% fines) are drawn as one Caltrans-style tile: the coarse soil's pattern with a lighter silt or clay pattern over it.

Other pairs, such as CL-ML (silty clay), ML/CL or SP-FILL, or a pair where one half is your own pattern, are drawn with the column split in half: the first symbol on the left, the second on the right. You can write a pair with - or /.

Materials and rock

To use one of these, set the layer's hatch to its code, for example "hatch": "SANDSTONE". They can also be paired with each other or with a USCS symbol: "hatch": "SP-FILL". Rock patterns follow the FGDC Digital Cartographic Standard for Geologic Map Symbolization (FGDC-STD-013-2006, section 37), drawn at a density that reads in a narrow column. A few related codes share a pattern with their group.
